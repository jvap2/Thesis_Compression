Both LUT$\times$LUT and decode-at-MAC/ingress \emph{accept} GF4's high-precision
magnitudes and pay to work around them. Fusing the Hadamard transform and the
operand decode into the GEMV keeps every 16-bit value in registers rather than
global memory, buying $4.3$--$6\times$ over a na\"ive unfused baseline
(\S\ref{sec:sw-support}) --- yet under $2\times$ against an all-fp16 kernel. The
gap that remains is not bandwidth, which the fused kernels have already removed,
but the decode itself: GF4's levels are arbitrary magnitudes, so reconstruction
is a lookup into a constant table that serializes on divergent codes on the GPU
and occupies a dedicated ROM on the ASIC. This lookup is common to \emph{every}
distribution-matched codebook, and it is what caps the fused path below
$2\times$. Fusion hides the traffic; it cannot remove the table.

To convert the format itself into the saving rather than emulating one around it,
we constrain the codebook to hardware-representable values --- dyadic levels with
bounded popcount, decodable by a short shift-and-add with no table and no
divergence --- and optimize \emph{within} that constraint. The na\"ive route,
rounding GF4's levels onto the nearest such grid, forfeits the accuracy that
motivated a distribution-matched codebook in the first place (the rounded
codebook is the weakest in our suite, Table~\ref{tab:codebook-suite}); we
therefore do not round a good codebook onto the lattice but \emph{solve} for the
best codebook \emph{on} it, so decode cost is discharged by construction and
accuracy is recovered rather than sacrificed. Because the objective is defined on
the RMS-normalized \emph{shape} of the operand distribution
(Eq.~\ref{eq:codebook-opt}) --- which the randomized Hadamard renders
approximately half-normal independent of the model --- the constrained optimum is
a property of the format, not of any one network: it is solved once, offline, by
exact enumeration over the lattice ($\binom{15}{6}=5005$ candidates) and reused
unchanged everywhere. The irregularity tax is paid once at design time instead of
on every operand at inference.
