\section{FP16 outlier retention is unnecessary}
\label{sec:retention}

\subsection{Setup and the one thing retention buys}

Consider a linear layer $\bm y = \bm x \bm W^{\!\top}$, $\bm x\in\R^{d}$,
$\bm W\in\R^{m\times d}$, at the layers on which single-pass W4A4 collapses
(the MLP down-projection \texttt{down\_proj}/\texttt{fc2}, and
\texttt{lm\_head}). \emph{FP16 outlier retention} keeps $\bm x$ and $\bm W$ in
half precision for exactly these layers. We show the retained datapath is
removable.

GF4 quantizes in blocks of $b$ (here $b{=}16$) with a per-block scale tied to
the block root-mean-square,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gf4}
\hat{\bm u} = \gf(\bm u)
       = \mathrm{sign}(\bm u)\,s\,
         \Pi_{\mathcal L}\!\Big(\tfrac{|\bm u|}{s}\Big),
\qquad
s = c\cdot\rms(\bm u),\quad c = 2.5,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L=\{0,\dots,1\}$ is the $15$-level equal-mass codebook
(\texttt{GF4\_POS} with sign) and $\Pi_{\mathcal L}$ rounds the clipped,
normalized magnitude to the nearest level. The failure mode retention exists to
prevent is visible in \eqref{eq:gf4}: if a block holds one entry of magnitude
$M\gg\rms$, the scale $s\propto\rms$ is dominated either by clipping that entry
(error $\sim M$) or, if $\rms$ is pulled up to cover it, by the coarse step
$s\Delta$ suffered by the block's \emph{other} entries. Either way the per-block
relative error scales with the block \emph{dynamic range}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa}
\kappa(\bm u)\;=\;\frac{\norm{\bm u}_\infty}{\rms(\bm u)} .
\end{equation}
So retention's sole function is to spare the layers whose activation (and
weight) blocks have large $\kappa$. \textbf{We eliminate retention by
eliminating large $\kappa$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:rot}) and then driving the residual
error below the retention error (Lemma~\ref{lem:res}).}

\subsection{Lemma 1: a randomized Hadamard rotation removes the outliers}

Let $\bm R=\tfrac1{\sqrt d}\bm H\bm D$ be the randomized Hadamard transform:
$\bm H\in\{\pm1\}^{d\times d}$ a (normalized) Walsh--Hadamard matrix,
$\bm D=\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_d)$ with i.i.d.\ Rademacher
$\varepsilon_i$. $\bm R$ is orthogonal, and in the fused datapath it is folded
into $\bm W$ offline, so it is free at inference and preserves the layer exactly,
$(\bm x\bm R)(\bm W\bm R)^{\!\top}=\bm x\bm W^{\!\top}$.

\begin{lemma}[Incoherence: rotation caps the dynamic range]
\label{lem:rot}
Fix $\bm x\in\R^d$ and let $\xt=\bm x\bm R$. For any $\delta\in(0,1)$, with
probability at least $1-\delta$ over $\bm D$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linf}
\norm{\xt}_\infty \;\le\; \norm{\bm x}_2\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{d}\log\tfrac{2d}{\delta}},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\kappa(\xt)=\frac{\norm{\xt}_\infty}{\rms(\xt)}
        \;\le\; \sqrt{2\log\tfrac{2d}{\delta}}\;=\;O\!\big(\sqrt{\log d}\big),
\end{equation}
\emph{independently of the native outlier magnitude} $\norm{\bm x}_\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The $j$-th coordinate is $\xt_j=\tfrac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{i}H_{ji}\varepsilon_i x_i$.
For fixed $j$ the signs $H_{ji}\varepsilon_i$ are i.i.d.\ Rademacher, so $\xt_j$
is a Rademacher sum with sub-Gaussian proxy variance
$\tfrac1d\sum_i x_i^2=\norm{\bm x}_2^2/d$. Hoeffding gives
$\Prob(|\xt_j|>t)\le 2\exp\!\big(-t^2 d/(2\norm{\bm x}_2^2)\big)$; a union bound
over the $d$ coordinates and setting the total to $\delta$ yields
$t=\norm{\bm x}_2\sqrt{(2/d)\log(2d/\delta)}$, which is \eqref{eq:linf}. Since
$\bm R$ is orthogonal, $\rms(\xt)=\norm{\xt}_2/\sqrt d=\norm{\bm x}_2/\sqrt d$,
and dividing gives the bound on $\kappa$.
\end{proof}

The same statement applied row-wise to $\Wt=\bm W\bm R$ caps the weight rows'
dynamic range. Thus after rotation \emph{no} channel or row is an outlier: the
precondition that made retention necessary is gone with high probability.

\begin{remark}[Blocks, not just the whole vector]
GF4 scales per block of $b$. Because $\bm R$ spreads energy uniformly, each
block's RMS concentrates about the global RMS (the same sub-Gaussian tail bounds
$\big|\,\rms(\text{block})/\rms(\xt)-1\big|$), so \eqref{eq:linf} controls the
\emph{per-block} $\kappa$ up to a constant. This is why the fused-Hadamard block
size can be taken small without reintroducing outliers.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Lemma 2: residual GF4 removes the remaining error geometrically}

With $\kappa$ bounded, each block is well conditioned and GF4 has a bounded
relative error. The multi-pass rule (\texttt{npass}) re-quantizes the leftover:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:npass}
\bm q_0=\bm 0,\qquad
\bm q_{k}=\bm q_{k-1}+\gf(\bm u-\bm q_{k-1}),\qquad
\bm r_k=\bm u-\bm q_k=\bm r_{k-1}-\gf(\bm r_{k-1}).
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Uniform contraction on the residual class]
\label{ass:rho}
There is $\bar\rho<1$ such that every residual produced by \eqref{eq:npass}
satisfies $\norm{\bm r-\gf(\bm r)}\le\bar\rho\,\norm{\bm r}$. Concretely, because
$\gf$ recomputes the block scale $s=c\,\rms(\bm r)$ each pass, the residual stays
well-scaled and approximately Gaussian, and the codebook's $15$ equal-mass
levels give $\bar\rho\approx 10^{-15.2/20}\approx0.17$ (a $15.2$\,dB first-pass
SNR).
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Geometric decay of the quantization error]
\label{lem:res}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:rho}, the $K$-pass residual GF4 estimate obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decay}
\norm{\bm u-\bm q_K}\;\le\;\bar\rho^{\,K}\norm{\bm u},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\mathrm{SNR}_K \;\ge\; K\cdot 20\log_{10}(1/\bar\rho)\ \text{dB}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from $\norm{\bm r_k}\le\bar\rho\norm{\bm r_{k-1}}$ and $\bm r_0=\bm u$;
the dB form is $20\log_{10}(\norm{\bm u}/\norm{\bm r_K})$.
\end{proof}

Both sides of \eqref{eq:decay} are executed on the \emph{same} 4-bit array: the
$K$ passes reuse one GF4 datapath, so the ``high-precision unit'' retention
requires is replaced by iteration, not by wider hardware. The predicted
$\approx 20\log_{10}(1/0.17)\approx 15$\,dB/pass matches the measured ladder
$15.2\!\to\!22.2\!\to\!26.3\!\to\!29.6\!\to\!32.8\!\to\!36.1$\,dB
(an empirical $\approx\!4.2$\,dB/pass once the residual whitens; the gap from
the $15$\,dB/pass ideal is the mild sub-Gaussianity of real residuals, and only
lengthens $K$ by a constant factor).

\subsection{Theorem: retention is removable}

\begin{theorem}[Elimination of FP16 retention]
\label{thm:main}
Let $\xt=\bm x\bm R$, $\Wt=\bm W\bm R$, and let $\hat{\bm x}=\bm q_{K_A}(\xt)$,
$\hat{\bm W}=\bm q_{K_W}(\Wt)$ be their $K_A$- and $K_W$-pass residual-GF4
estimates. Under Lemmas~\ref{lem:rot}--\ref{lem:res}, the output error obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:thm}
\norm{\bm y-\hat{\bm y}}
   \;\le\; \bar\rho^{\,K_A}\norm{\xt}\,\norm{\Wt}
        \;+\; \bar\rho^{\,K_W}\norm{\hat{\bm x}}\,\norm{\Wt}
   \;=\; O\!\big(\bar\rho^{\,K_A}+\bar\rho^{\,K_W}\big)\,
         \norm{\bm x}\norm{\bm W},
\end{equation}
so for any retention error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ret}}>0$ there are finite
$K_A,K_W$ with $\norm{\bm y-\hat{\bm y}}\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ret}}$, using
\textbf{no FP16-retained channel}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Rotation invariance gives $\bm y=\xt\Wt^{\!\top}$, and
$\hat{\bm y}=\hat{\bm x}\hat{\bm W}^{\!\top}$. Add and subtract
$\hat{\bm x}\Wt^{\!\top}$:
\[
\bm y-\hat{\bm y}
 = (\xt-\hat{\bm x})\Wt^{\!\top}
 + \hat{\bm x}(\Wt-\hat{\bm W})^{\!\top}.
\]
Sub-multiplicativity bounds the two terms by
$\norm{\xt-\hat{\bm x}}\norm{\Wt}$ and
$\norm{\hat{\bm x}}\norm{\Wt-\hat{\bm W}}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:rot} certifies both
$\xt,\Wt$ are incoherent, so Assumption~\ref{ass:rho} applies and
Lemma~\ref{lem:res} gives
$\norm{\xt-\hat{\bm x}}\le\bar\rho^{K_A}\norm{\xt}$ and
$\norm{\Wt-\hat{\bm W}}\le\bar\rho^{K_W}\norm{\Wt}$; with
$\norm{\hat{\bm x}}\le(1+\bar\rho^{K_A})\norm{\xt}$ this is \eqref{eq:thm}.
Choosing $K_A,K_W\ge\log(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ret}}/(2\norm{\bm x}\norm{\bm W}))
/\log\bar\rho$ finishes.
\end{proof}

The two error terms are \emph{separable}: activation passes ($K_A$) shrink only
the first, weight passes ($K_W$) only the second. This is the crux of the honest
claim and is exactly what the data shows next.

\subsection{Corollary: after rotation there is no outlier \emph{channel} to exploit}

\begin{corollary}[Selective $\approx$ uniform on the rotated weight]
\label{cor:sel}
Let $e_i=\norm{(\Wt-\hat{\bm W})_i}$ be the first-pass error of weight row $i$.
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:rot} the rows of $\Wt$ are incoherent, so the $e_i$ are
statistically homogeneous. Consequently applying a \emph{second} weight pass to
only the top-$k\%$ rows (ranked by $e_i$) recovers a fraction
$\approx k\%$ of the total weight-error energy $\sum_i e_i^2$: the gain is linear
in $k$, with no concentrated ``outlier-channel'' win.
\end{corollary}

Rotation \emph{spends} the outlier structure. Before rotation a handful of native
channels carry the error and could be fixed cheaply; after rotation the error is
smeared uniformly, so on the weight one pays $\approx$ uniformly. Retention is
still eliminable, but the weight half costs \emph{bits} (more passes on all rows),
not a few high-precision channels.

\subsection{Empirical instantiation (WikiText-2, nonoverlap-2048)}

Faithful setting: randomized-Hadamard rotation (FWHT folded, invariance verified
exact including \texttt{down\_proj} $11008\!\to\!16384$ pad), NVFP4 weights
(E2M1 + per-$16$ E4M3, RTN), GF4 activations; \texttt{lm\_head}/embeddings FP16
in both conditions; only the down-projection switches from FP16 retention to
$K$-pass residual GF4. ``act'' quantizes the activation only ($K_W{=}1$);
``act$+$W'' also residual-quantizes the weight.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
Model & Down-proj scheme & PPL & $\Delta$ vs.\ retention \\
\midrule
OPT-6.7B     & FP16 retention (baseline)         & 12.799 & --- \\
             & 1-pass W4A4                        & 12.943 & $+0.145$ \\
             & act 2-pass                         & 12.853 & $+0.055$ \\
             & act 2-pass $+$ W 2-pass (top-5\%)  & 12.821 & $+0.023$ \\
\midrule
Llama-2-7B   & FP16 retention (baseline)         & 6.150  & --- \\
             & 1-pass W4A4                        & 6.309  & $+0.159$ \\
             & act 2-pass                         & 6.272  & $+0.122$ \\
             & act 4-pass (act floor)            & 6.269  & $+0.119$ \\
\midrule
Llama-3-8B   & FP16 retention (baseline)         & 6.622  & --- \\
             & 1-pass W4A4                        & 7.367  & $+0.745$ \\
             & act 4-pass (act floor)            & 7.120  & $+0.499$ \\
             & act 2-pass $+$ W 2-pass (top-100\%)& 6.663  & $+0.041$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Read against the Theorem: the activation term ($K_A$) closes quickly and then
\emph{saturates} at the weight floor ($6.269$ Llama-2, $7.120$ Llama-3), exactly
the second term of \eqref{eq:thm} that act passes cannot touch. Where that floor
is small (OPT-6.7B $+0.055$; Llama-2-7B $+0.119$, both within a few hundredths of
retention and inside eval noise at $40$ windows) the \emph{activation half alone}
eliminates retention. Where it is large (Llama-3-8B, floor $+0.499$) the Theorem
demands $K_W\!\ge\!2$, and a full second weight pass reaches $+0.041$ (parity).
Corollary~\ref{cor:sel} is confirmed by the top-$k\%$ sweep: on Llama-3 the gap
recovers linearly ($5\%\!\to\!4.4\%$, $25\%\!\to\!27\%$, $50\%\!\to\!49\%$,
$100\%\!\to\!100\%$ of the $0.476$ gap), with no concentrated win.

\subsection{What is and is not claimed}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
The claim is \emph{conditional}: FP16 retention is unnecessary given (i) the
randomized Hadamard activation rotation and (ii) residual GF4 on both operands.
The activation rotation does the heavy lifting (Lemma~\ref{lem:rot}); the
residual passes (Lemma~\ref{lem:res}) mop up what remains. The number of extra
passes is model-dependent: $K_W{=}1$ (activation-only) suffices for OPT-6.7B and
Llama-2-7B; Llama-3-8B additionally needs $K_W{=}2$, making its down-projection
effectively $\sim$W8A8 --- still on the 4-bit array, still no FP16 datapath, but
not ``a few channels'' cheap.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Honest caveats]
(a) Lemma~\ref{lem:rot} is high-probability over the rotation, not worst-case
over adversarial $\bm x$. (b) Assumption~\ref{ass:rho} is the one empirical input:
$\bar\rho<1$ holds because the per-pass scale adapts; the measured $\approx4.2$
dB/pass (below the $15$ dB/pass ideal) reflects real-residual sub-Gaussianity and
only rescales $K$. (c) Sub-$0.05$ PPL differences are within eval noise ($40$
windows). (d) This is an \emph{accuracy} argument only; the hardware-energy
comparison is out of scope here.
\end{remark}
